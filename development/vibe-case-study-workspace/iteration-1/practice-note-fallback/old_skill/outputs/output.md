# Case-study diagnosis

**Recommended format:** Practice Note, contingent on finding one evidence-backed build lesson

**Confidence:** High that a full portfolio case study is not supported; medium that a publishable Practice Note can be supported after a short context interview

**Reason a full case study is not supported:** The project is unfinished, has only been used twice by its creator, and has no external users, feedback, packaged release, tested alternatives, or documented product decision. Those two personal uses show that the script ran in a real workflow; they are not adoption or independent validation. A Product Utility case would need a specific problem, a complete working experience, at least one consequential scope or interaction decision, and a real-use or iteration receipt. The available evidence does not meet that gate.

**Alternative format:** Keep it as a private project note if there is no concrete failure, comparison, or judgment behind the build. A public Practice Note should not be created from the artifact alone.

**Selected variant:** Build lesson

**Provisional Story Thesis:** I built a command-line script to rename exported screenshots and used it in two of my own workflows. The publishable lesson is still unknown: the note needs one concrete account of what the manual process made difficult, what changed in the script, and what those two runs demonstrated.

This thesis is deliberately provisional. It describes the artifact and the known use boundary without claiming that the script saved time, improved quality, or solved a problem for anyone else.

## Evidence map

**Observed evidence:** None yet. No relevant repository files, history, documentation, sample inputs, or outputs were available to inspect with the request.

**Author-reported evidence:**

- The repository contains an unfinished command-line script.
- The script renames exported screenshots.
- You used it twice yourself.
- There are no other users and no feedback.
- There is no packaged release.
- No alternatives were evaluated.
- No product decision has been documented.

**Inferred, not publishable as fact:**

- Manual screenshot renaming may have been repetitive or error-prone.
- A naming convention may have mattered to a downstream portfolio, documentation, or handoff workflow.
- The second use may indicate personal reuse, but it does not establish reliability or broader usefulness.

**Missing context:**

- The concrete moment or recurring task that triggered the script.
- The exact before-state workflow and what was unsatisfactory about it.
- A failure, constraint, version change, or implementation choice that required judgment.
- What happened in each of the two runs, including whether the output was correct and what still required manual work.
- Inspectable before-and-after filenames or terminal output.
- What you decided versus what AI or another tool generated.
- The conditions where this approach is useful and where it is not.
- Any complete task flow, external use, feedback, packaging, release, or distribution evidence that could later support a full Product Utility case.

## Recommended length

**Recommendation:** Compact, 450–650 words, if the missing trigger, contrast, and judgment can be supplied. One bounded build lesson is the most this evidence can carry without padding. Do not draft yet: you have not selected or delegated a length, and the Practice Note evidence gate is not complete.

**Length options for selection:**

- **Compact — 450–650 words:** Recommended. Best for one trigger, one change or constraint, evidence from the two creator-run uses, and one bounded lesson.
- **Standard — 800–1,100 words:** Not currently supported. It would require meaningful evolution, two or three consequential decisions, and receipts showing iteration or validation.
- **Extended — 1,300–1,800 words:** Not appropriate for the current project. It would require multiple phases, alternatives, richer constraints, and substantial evidence.

## Proposed narrative modules

If the evidence gate is met, the Practice Note should use this narrow progression:

1. **The renaming task that triggered the script** — the actual export context and why manual naming mattered.
2. **What the first run exposed** — a concrete failure, awkward step, or constraint, if one occurred.
3. **What changed before the second run** — only if repository history or your account can verify a change and its rationale.
4. **What the two runs do and do not prove** — personal reuse and inspectable output, with no claim of adoption or general reliability.
5. **Where the method applies** — a bounded recommendation and the situations that still require manual review.

Do not add Research, Personas, Ideation, Usability Testing, or Impact sections unless evidence of those activities is supplied.

## Candidate proof blocks

No impact or adoption block is currently supportable. After verification, two modest blocks may be possible:

> **Constraint**  
> [Verified description of the screenshot-export naming problem]

> **Evidence**  
> Used in two creator-run workflows; no external-use validation yet.

The number “two” may document the basis of personal reuse, but it should not appear in an Adoption or Impact block.

## Visual Evidence Plan

1. **Trigger evidence** — capture a real export folder before renaming. It must show the original filenames and enough context to explain the task. Do not reconstruct it unless clearly labeled.
2. **Contrast** — show the same files before and after one verified run. Preserve the exact filename transformation; do not claim time savings without a consistent measurement.
3. **Method** — use a compact, clearly labeled workflow: exported screenshots → script input or command → renamed files → manual review. Confirm every step before publishing it.
4. **Result** — capture terminal output and the resulting directory from one of the two real runs, including any errors or manual corrections.

If no original artifacts remain, use a text reconstruction labeled “Reconstructed from the author’s account,” not generated replacement product UI.

## Claims to avoid or soften

- Do not call the script launched, released, packaged, production-ready, or reliable.
- Do not describe two creator-run uses as adoption, validation, or user success.
- Do not claim users, feedback-led iteration, market need, or business impact.
- Do not claim the script saved time, reduced errors, or improved consistency unless a before/after basis can be shown.
- Do not claim a deliberate product decision until the decision, alternatives considered, and rationale are documented.
- Do not call it a reusable workflow or system while it remains an unpackaged personal script.
- Do not describe AI activity as design judgment unless a specific constraint, correction, or human–AI handoff can be evidenced.

## What would upgrade this to a full case study

A Product Utility case could become supportable if the project gains a complete demonstrable task flow, one documented scope or interaction decision, a packaged way for someone else to use it, and at least one external-use, feedback, repeat-use, or evidence-triggered version-change receipt. Availability alone would not count as adoption.
