# Evidence-safe direction

I would not use Meng’s attendee-teaching result as this project’s Adoption block. That behavior belongs to Meng’s case, and transferring it would turn another project’s evidence into an unsupported claim about the AI research summarizer.

The available evidence supports two narrower statements: the repository shows that a functioning product was built, and the launch post’s 60 likes show public interest. Neither establishes use, reuse, teaching, invitation, redistribution, or second-order adoption.

## Pre-draft decision package

**Recommended archetype:** Product Utility, provisionally. The summarizer appears to be a focused tool, but the specific user, research situation, and product decision still need confirmation.

**Confidence:** Medium-low. There is enough evidence to discuss the shipped output and launch response, but not enough to prove usefulness or adoption.

**Story thesis:** I turned an AI-assisted research-synthesis workflow into a working summarizer and attracted initial launch interest, while leaving actual use and adoption as outcomes still to be validated.

**Selected length:** Compact, 400–500 words.

**Observed evidence:** A working implementation exists in the repository, assuming its complete task flow is inspectable.

**Author-reported evidence:** The launch post received 60 likes. A screenshot or link should be retained as the receipt.

**Missing evidence:** The concrete trigger; intended user; prior workflow; one consequential scope or interaction decision; independent task completion; repeat use; feedback-led change; and any teaching, invitation, contribution, or redistribution.

**Relevant source pattern:** MX-11, “Second-order use is stronger than raw reach.” It is useful only for defining what to look for next, not for supplying this project’s result.

**Adapted candidate statement:** A meaningful future adoption signal would be a user returning to the summarizer and introducing or teaching the workflow to someone else without my facilitation.

**Candidate status:** Hypothesis. Keep it as a future success signal or research question; do not publish it as something that happened and do not give it proof styling.

**Primary proof-statement specification:** None yet. A functioning repository is output evidence, and 60 likes are an interest signal; neither is strong enough to support an outcome or adoption callout.

**Claims to avoid:** “Teams adopted the workflow,” “users taught it to colleagues,” “the summarizer spread organically,” “60 people used it,” or any wording that equates likes with adoption.

## Compact public outline

1. **Hero:** Name the summarizer, the specific research task it supports, and the intended user once confirmed.
2. **Before state:** Show the concrete research-synthesis friction without inventing a broader market need.
3. **Working product:** Demonstrate the complete input-to-summary flow from the repository.
4. **One pivotal decision:** Add the strongest documented choice about source handling, synthesis, traceability, or human review.
5. **What the launch showed:** Use the evidence-safe block below.
6. **Next proof:** State that independent use, return behavior, and user-led sharing are the next validation signals.

## Suggested public block

### What the launch showed

I shipped a working AI research summarizer, and the launch post received 60 likes. That response showed interest in the idea, not adoption of the workflow. I do not yet have evidence that anyone completed a research task with it, returned to use it, taught it to a team, invited another user, or redistributed it. Those behaviors are the next signals I would track.

A full 400–500-word Compact case would currently require invented context or padded process language. The evidence-safe deliverable is therefore this Compact outline plus the launch-interest block. Once the trigger, pivotal decision, and one real-use receipt are available, it can support a complete case without borrowing Meng’s outcome.
