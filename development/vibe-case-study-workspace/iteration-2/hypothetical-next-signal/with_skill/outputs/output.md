# Pre-draft decision package

Recommended archetype: **Workflow / System**, provisionally. An internal onboarding checklist is most compelling as a reusable team workflow rather than as a standalone interface. This archetype will hold only if the checklist encodes a repeatable onboarding process, has a real output or completed instance, and addresses friction that occurred across more than one colleague or onboarding cycle.

Confidence: **Medium.** The reported use is stronger than a proposal: eight colleagues completed facilitated walkthroughs, and three later used the checklist again. However, the evidence does not yet establish self-serve use, distribution beyond the creator, contribution, or team ownership.

Alternative: **Product Utility** if the checklist mainly helps an individual complete one bounded task and does not encode a broader team workflow. Use a **Practice Note** if there is no concrete repeated before-state, packaged artifact, or inspectable example of the checklist in use.

Story thesis: I turned recurring onboarding steps into a checklist colleagues could use across more than one session; facilitated walkthroughs established initial usability, while three returning users provided an early repeat-use signal without yet proving that the workflow belongs to the team.

## Length options

- **Compact — 400–500 words:** Best if the evidence supports one clear progression: recurring onboarding friction, the checklist decision, facilitated walkthroughs, and the return-use signal.
- **Standard — 500–600 words:** Recommended if there is a visible before workflow, at least one consequential revision prompted by a walkthrough, and receipts for both the checklist and repeat use.
- **Expanded — 700–800 words:** Worthwhile only if there are several documented versions, stakeholder or access constraints, a concrete packaging or distribution decision, and enough evidence to explain where human judgment remains necessary.

Recommended length: **Standard, provisionally.** The walkthrough-to-return sequence can sustain more than a short launch note, but Expanded would overreach without richer version history, constraints, and distribution evidence. Choose a mode before drafting; if no feedback-led change or before-state receipt exists, Compact is the safer choice.

## Evidence map

Observed evidence:

- None supplied as independently inspectable evidence in the prompt. The checklist, completed instances, usage records, feedback, and version history still need to be reviewed before any claim is classified as Observed.

Author-reported evidence:

- The artifact is an internal onboarding checklist.
- Eight colleagues completed facilitated walkthroughs.
- Three colleagues returned to use it again.
- Nobody has taught the workflow, invited new users, contributed to it, or redistributed it.

Inferred:

- Onboarding friction recurred across people or cycles.
- The three returning colleagues used the checklist independently and in real onboarding work.
- The checklist reduced dependence on its creator.
- The facilitated walkthroughs caused a particular revision.

Unknown / missing evidence:

- The old onboarding sequence, its repeated failure points, and who experienced them.
- What judgment or decision rules the checklist encodes rather than merely lists.
- Whether the three return uses were independent, tied to real work, and completed without assistance.
- What changed between versions and which colleague feedback caused the change.
- How the checklist is packaged, accessed, maintained, and handed off.
- A real input/completed-output pair and an anonymizable usage receipt.
- Ownership across the author, collaborators, AI, and checklist users.
- The workflow's limitations and cases that still need live guidance.

Adoption level: **Reuse, provisionally and author-reported.** Three people returned after their facilitated walkthroughs. Describe this as repeat use only after confirming that they applied the checklist again themselves. The eight walkthrough completions are facilitated trials, not self-serve adoption. There is no evidence of contribution or second-order adoption.

## Recommended narrative spine

1. **Onboarding kept depending on live guidance** — establish the recurring people, steps, and breakdowns.
2. **I encoded the sequence into a checklist** — show the decisions and judgment embedded in it, not just its sections.
3. **Eight colleagues walked through it with me** — describe these accurately as facilitated trials and show what was learned.
4. **Three returned after the walkthrough** — establish whether the return was independent, for real work, and what they completed.
5. **The next test is transfer, not more guided reach** — define teaching, invitation, contribution, or redistribution as future success signals.

Selected middle modules: Before Workflow; Encoding the Judgment; Workflow Walkthrough; Packaging; Adoption Loop; Boundary.

## Visual receipts required

- A before-workflow diagram showing people, handoffs, repeated steps, and where live guidance was required.
- An anonymized checklist screen or artifact with the encoded decisions annotated.
- One real input/completed-checklist output pair.
- A version comparison tied to a specific walkthrough request, if such a change occurred.
- An anonymized receipt showing the three return uses, including whether they occurred without facilitation.
- The access or packaging surface showing how a colleague finds and starts the checklist.
- Later, if it happens, an invitation, teaching artifact, contribution trail, or redistribution receipt for second-order adoption.

Do not create replacement product UI to simulate missing evidence. Use real captures or a clearly labeled reconstruction of the workflow for explanation only.

## Claims to avoid or qualify

- Do not say the checklist was adopted by the team; current evidence supports facilitated trial and possible repeat use, not team ownership.
- Do not call eight walkthrough completions self-serve adoption.
- Do not say the workflow spreads, scales, or no longer depends on the creator.
- Do not imply that the three return users taught, invited, contributed, or redistributed it.
- Do not claim efficiency, consistency, or reduced onboarding time without a comparable baseline and receipt.
- Do not describe return use as independent until that is confirmed.
- Do not claim a feedback-led product change unless a request and visible revision can be paired.
- Do not use the hypothetical team-ownership threshold as a highlighted proof statement.

## Minimum context interview before drafting

1. What repeated onboarding problem did the checklist replace, for whom, and what did the old sequence require?
2. Did the three returning colleagues use it independently for real onboarding work, and what evidence can be anonymized?
3. Which walkthrough comment changed a specific checklist step, rule, or version?
4. What judgment did you encode, and where must a person still interpret or intervene?
5. How do colleagues access the checklist now, and what still requires you to explain, approve, or maintain?
6. What did you personally define, what did collaborators contribute, and did AI materially shape the artifact or working method?

Drafting should begin only after the author chooses Compact, Standard, or Expanded (or explicitly delegates the choice) and answers the questions needed to support the selected length.
