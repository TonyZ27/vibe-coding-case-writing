# Workflow / System Template Contract

## Purpose

Use this contract when a project turns repeated team or organizational friction into a reusable workflow, skill, tool, framework, or learning system. The case should prove that the capability travels beyond its creator.

Default audience: product-design recruiter or hiring manager.  
Default output: a concise case study readable in roughly five minutes.  
Reference basis: [Meng Xie AI-Native Case Study Pattern Matrix](../../../meng-case-study-pattern-matrix-validated.md).

## Use this archetype when

- The starting problem recurs across projects, people, or teams.
- The output contains repeatable steps, decision rules, reusable tooling, or encoded expertise.
- At least one real input/output pair exists.
- Someone other than the creator has used, installed, contributed to, taught, or referenced the capability—or the author can provide that evidence.
- The primary value is leverage and repeatability rather than one end-user interaction.

Typical examples include internal tools, Agents, Claude skills, automation, audit workflows, design-system utilities, playbooks, and organizational learning systems.

## Do not use this archetype when

- The strongest evidence is usefulness to a narrow end user. Use **Product Utility**.
- The central story is choosing a product direction under real constraints. Use **Product Experiment**.
- The workflow solved only one isolated problem and has no reusable structure or adoption evidence. Use **Practice Note**.
- “Reusable” is only an aspiration with no executable steps, packaged artifact, or real output.
- The case relies solely on a launch post, reaction count, or the creator's own repeated use.

## What the case must prove

By the end, a reviewer should be able to answer:

1. What repeated friction affected whom?
2. Why did existing tools or handoffs fail?
3. What is the new workflow or system model?
4. Where do human judgment and AI or automation each operate?
5. What real output does the system produce?
6. How was the workflow packaged or distributed?
7. What evidence shows that it travels beyond the creator?
8. What boundary, failure, or trade-off limits its use?

## Minimum evidence gate

| Required evidence | Passing condition | If missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated friction | More than one occurrence, user, project, or team is identified | Ask the author; otherwise route to Practice Note |
| Workflow model | Steps, inputs, decisions, and outputs can be described | Do not substitute a tool list |
| Real output | At least one artifact generated by the workflow exists | Label the system proposed, not operational |
| Packaging | Skill, command, tool, playbook, template, or reproducible instructions exist | Describe as personal workflow |
| Adoption | Another person used, reused, contributed to, or taught it | Do not claim adoption; label reactions as interest |
| Ownership | Creator, AI, collaborators, and adopters are distinguishable | Ask before drafting |

## Story Thesis

### Formula

> I turned **[repeated friction]** into **[reusable capability]** by encoding **[judgment or workflow]**, enabling **[other people or teams]** to achieve **[concrete outcome]** without depending on **[old bottleneck]**.

### Strong example

> I reversed the production-to-Figma handoff into a reusable skill, enabling designers to reconstruct editable source files and documented flows without manually piecing together screens, code, and remembered intent.

### Weak example

> I built an AI tool that saves designers time.

The weak version does not define the old workflow, encoded judgment, output, or adoption.

## Recommended narrative modules

The fixed shell is:

1. **Hero claim** — recurring friction + reusable capability + who benefits.
2. **At a Glance** — What / How / Why it matters.
3. **Context** — the repeated before workflow and its cost.
4. **System middle** — modules selected from the evidence.
5. **Packaging and adoption** — how the capability traveled.
6. **Boundary and transferable pattern** — where it works, where it does not, and what was learned.

Choose three to five middle modules:

- **Before Workflow** — actors, steps, handoffs, and failure points.
- **System Model** — triggers, inputs, decisions, automated actions, human judgment, and outputs.
- **Workflow Walkthrough** — one real end-to-end run.
- **Encoding the Judgment** — rules, lenses, constraints, or expertise embedded in the capability.
- **Packaging** — skill, command, tool, playbook, template, or distribution method.
- **Distribution Trade-offs** — friction, access, security, maintenance, or platform choices.
- **Adoption Loop** — install, reuse, contribution, teaching, or second-order adoption.
- **Failure / Boundary** — unsuitable tasks, required manual intervention, or known limitations.

Avoid presenting architecture for its own sake. Include only technical detail that explains safety, repeatability, trade-offs, or adoption.

## Context interview

Quick Mode should select three to six questions the repository cannot answer:

- Where did this friction repeat, and who experienced it?
- What did the old workflow require from people?
- Which judgment did you encode rather than automate blindly?
- What can a new user produce without your help?
- How was the workflow packaged and distributed?
- Who used or reused it, and what did they do afterward?
- What failed during adoption or created setup friction?
- Where does the workflow still require manual judgment?
- What did AI execute, and what did you personally define, validate, or correct?
- What evidence can be shown publicly or anonymized?

## Visual Evidence Plan

### Required visual package

1. **Before workflow** — real steps, handoffs, and friction points.
2. **New system flow** — trigger → input → automated action → human judgment → output.
3. **Real input/output** — inspectable source material and generated artifact.
4. **Packaging** — command, skill, tool UI, playbook, or installation surface.
5. **Adoption receipt** — reuse, contribution, installation, teaching, or second-order sharing.

### Conditional visuals

- Distribution decision matrix when channels materially affect adoption.
- Failure or manual-handoff comparison when full automation did not work.
- Contributor map for community-built systems.
- Before/after setup time only when the same task and measurement basis are confirmed.
- Architecture diagram only when it clarifies ownership, data movement, or a system boundary.

### Workflow-diagram rules

- Show people and decisions, not only tools.
- Distinguish automatic actions from human judgment.
- Mark inferred or reconstructed steps.
- Show failure or fallback paths when they matter.
- Do not imply target-client compatibility, security approval, or organizational adoption from automated tests alone.

## Adoption evidence ladder

Use the strongest level actually supported:

1. **Proposed:** Creator documented a reusable approach; no external use yet.
2. **Interest:** Reactions, comments, requests, or sign-ups.
3. **Trial:** Someone else installed or ran it once.
4. **Reuse:** Someone applied it to another project without the creator doing the work.
5. **Contribution:** Others added features, content, rules, or improvements.
6. **Second-order adoption:** A user taught or distributed the workflow to others.

Never describe levels 1–2 as adoption.

## Common overclaiming risks

- Calling a personal workflow a system because it has multiple steps.
- Calling a workflow reusable without another user's successful run.
- Treating a launch post as adoption.
- Claiming hours-to-minutes improvement without a consistent task baseline.
- Claiming “org-wide” when availability and repeated use are not both evidenced.
- Treating a generated output as correct without review or target-environment validation.
- Hiding manual cleanup that is essential to output quality.
- Describing encoded prompts as design expertise without showing the rules or judgment they contain.

## Quality rubric

Score each dimension 0–2. A publishable draft requires at least 12/14 and no zero in Workflow Model, Adoption Integrity, Evidence Integrity, or Ownership.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated friction | One isolated event | Repetition claimed | Actors and repeated costs are concrete |
| Workflow model | Tool list | Steps are shown | Inputs, decisions, actions, outputs, and fallbacks are clear |
| Encoded judgment | Automation described | Rules are named | Human expertise and limits are inspectable |
| Real output | Hypothetical output | One output shown | Input/output quality and review are visible |
| Adoption integrity | Reactions called adoption | Trial or interest labeled correctly | Reuse, contribution, or second-order adoption is evidenced |
| Evidence integrity | Claims exceed receipts | Most claims are labeled | Claims, receipts, and gaps are explicit |
| Ownership | Roles blurred | Contributions summarized | Creator, AI, collaborators, and adopters are separated |
