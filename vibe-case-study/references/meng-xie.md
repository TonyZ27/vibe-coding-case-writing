## Useful principles

### Keep the Hero concise

Introduce the artifact, what is different about it, and why it matters. The Hero should create orientation, not summarize the entire process or lead with an AI tool.

### Use At a Glance for orientation

Role, audience, state, timeframe, and contribution can help a reader enter the story quickly. Keep this information secondary to the case's argument.

### Keep Context short

A specific trigger or constraint is often more credible than a broad market claim. Explain enough of the prior situation to make the product decision legible, then move forward.

### Let the middle follow the project

The strongest cases use sections named after actual decisions, tasks, constraints, or stages. They do not need to reproduce a standard design-process sequence.

Examples of useful narrative moves include:

- a personal constraint that sharply defined scope;
- a technical boundary that changed the interaction;
- a real-state prototype that exposed a weak direction;
- feedback or contribution that expanded the product;
- distribution friction that became part of the experience;
- repeat or second-order use that showed a workflow traveling beyond its creator.

These are prompts for investigation, not required patterns or project categories.

### Build sections around arguments

Each section should make a claim and support it with product behavior, a decision, an iteration, a result, or a visual receipt. Process documentation is useful only when it explains why the product changed.

### Extract a reusable insight only when earned

A broader belief can close the case when the body establishes the causal chain that taught it. If the lesson could be pasted onto any project, it is probably too generic.

### Vary depth without changing the reading model

A small utility can tell one compact decision story. A multi-stage experiment can support a deeper case. Both can retain the same Hero, At a Glance, Context, project-specific middle, Outcome, and optional Resources.

## Evidence boundaries

- Do not borrow another case's user behavior, impact, adoption, or outcome.
- Teaching, invitation, redistribution, and use without creator facilitation can support second-order adoption only when they occurred in the current project.
- Facilitated trials, repeat use, views, likes, and comments are different signals; describe each literally.
- Use reference examples to improve questions or structure, never to fill missing facts.
